## Design Specificity Verdict

The interface has an authored foundation: a large uninterrupted canvas, Surface/Space wording, curated starter brushes, safe-area-aware mobile layout, and a quiet cyan-led interaction system fit the product. The deeper control taxonomy is less specific and slips toward a generic 3D editor. The largest missed opportunity is to organize the entire experience around the two human goals—**Draw in space** and **Paint a model**—instead of exposing the implementation categories Draw, Select, Add, Layers, placement, profiles, materials, gizmos, guides, and scene settings simultaneously.

## Overall Impression

This is closer to a friendly sketchbook than a cockpit, but it is not yet effortlessly simple. The core shell is well-intentioned; the main improvement is to turn a feature-oriented interface into a task-oriented one. A new user should make one choice first—draw freely or paint a model—and only then see the few controls relevant to that choice.

## What's Working

- **The responsive foundation is sound.** `100dvh`, safe-area insets, a phone bottom sheet, a tablet side inspector, reduced-motion handling, visible focus styles, and a global 44px button floor show deliberate touch-first work.
- **Core language is often excellent.** “Surface,” “Space,” “Flat,” “Round,” “Draw,” “Erase,” and “Layers” are understandable without 3D training.
- **Several complexity controls already exist.** Four essential brushes are shown before “More brushes”; transform details and secondary Preferences are collapsible; destructive layer changes use confirmation; undo/redo is persistent.

## Cognitive Load

Five of eight checks fail: single focus, chunking, visual hierarchy, one thing at a time, and minimal choices. Grouping and working-memory support are generally good; progressive disclosure is present but applied too late.

Key overload points:

- The workspace exposes 4 persistent modes plus 6 persistent quick controls before counting the top bar.
- Draw > Color exposes 8 swatches, a full picker, and 4 Looks in one view.
- Select exposes 5 target scopes plus multiple toggles, actions, and transform details.
- Preferences exposes roughly 10 rows before “More settings,” including four navigator styles, three sensitivity choices, projection, dock placement, radial menu, sound, navigator visibility, and grid.
- A selected layer can reveal several structural actions plus 8 color tags and opacity.

## Emotional Journey

The opening can feel inviting because the artwork stays central and the defaults are usable. The emotional valley arrives when users open Draw, Select, or Preferences and encounter dense terminology and many equally weighted controls. The end of the journey is also weaker than it should be: export offers useful real outputs, but the everyday reassurance “your work is saved” is invisible. The ideal peak-end sequence is: satisfying first stroke, clear saved confirmation, then a simple “Image / 3D file / AR” finish.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The primary workspace still asks users to process too many controls

**Why it matters:** Four mode buttons, six quick controls, five top-bar actions, and a corner navigator create several simultaneous zones of attention. This contradicts the promise of an effortless first stroke.

**Fix:** Keep the top bar to Project, Undo, Redo, a visible Saved state, and More. Move Illumination under More > Scene. Keep one compact tool rail and make the bottom strip contextual. While drawing, show only: **On model / In space**, Brush, Color, and Size. Put Opacity inside Color or Brush. Show Guide only after a guide exists. Do not expose inactive-context controls.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P1] Work protection exists but its reassurance is hidden

**Why it matters:** “My work will not be lost” is a core product promise. `AutoSaveToast` defines Saving, Saved, and Error messages, but `ProResponsive.css` hides `#mody-autosave-indicator` entirely. Users must open advanced Preferences to discover storage and recovery.

**Fix:** Add a quiet persistent state beside the project name: **Saving…**, **Saved**, or **Couldn’t save — tap to fix**. Let Saved fade to a subtle check, but never hide an error. Keep “Protect this device” and “Restore backup” in a dedicated Project & recovery sheet, not Diagnostics.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Task language gives way to 3D-editor language in deeper panels

**Why it matters:** First-timers can understand Surface/Space, then are asked to translate Conformal, Non-Conf, Transform Gizmo, isometric, projection, falloff, negative mask, and engine latency. This makes the product feel like simplified Blender.

**Fix:** Rename or demote technical terms: **Transform Gizmo → Move handles**, **Conformal → Follows the surface**, **Non-Conf → Free stroke**, **Camera Projection → View style**, **Perspective / Orthographic → Natural depth / Flat view**, **Soft Selection Falloff → Move nearby parts**, **Performance Diagnostics → Performance info**, **Negative Mask → Cut through stroke**. Keep X/Y/Z and units under an explicit **Precise controls** disclosure.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The information architecture is feature-first instead of goal-first

**Why it matters:** The two defining jobs—drawing in air and painting a model—are represented by a small placement control. Loading a model is buried in Add, while settings also contains projects and export. Beginners have to construct the workflow themselves.

**Fix:** Use an empty-canvas start card with two large choices: **Draw in space** and **Paint a model**. The second opens recent/built-in/import options. Keep creation tools under Add, but move Project/Recovery and Share/Export out of Preferences into top-level More groups. Preferences should contain only Appearance, Input, and Workspace; advanced navigation/display options sit under Advanced.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P2] Touch and accessibility details undercut an otherwise good foundation

**Why it matters:** Mode labels are visually hidden, swatches explicitly override minimum sizing, shared sheet close buttons are forced to 38px, settings helper text is 9–9.5px, and general Studio sheets do not establish/restore focus like the More dialog does. These are material on a Galaxy Tab and for keyboard/screen-reader users.

**Fix:** Keep all controls at least 44×44px; use 48px for primary tablet actions. Raise helper copy to 12px minimum and body copy to 14–16px. Show mode labels on tablet or reveal a labeled selected state. Add explicit accessible names to icon-only destructive buttons. Give StudioSheet initial focus, a focus boundary, and focus restoration; use `aria-labelledby` with its visible heading.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan, first-time 3D creator:** Jordan can recognize Draw, but has no strong first-step choice between drawing in space and painting a model. Opening Draw introduces 8 swatches, Looks, profiles, smoothing, snapping, and technical labels. Jordan is likely to experiment randomly rather than form a reliable mental model.

**Casey, distracted tablet user:** The persistent zones are reachable, but ten workspace controls plus a top bar demand too much scanning. Tiny 9–10px labels and 38px sheet-close targets are fragile during one-handed or interrupted use. Autosave being invisible removes the reassurance Casey needs after switching apps.

**Sam, accessibility-dependent creator:** Global focus styles and 44px defaults are good, but exceptions remain. Icon-only mode labels rely on accessible names without visible reinforcement; hover titles do not work on touch; some sheets lack full focus management; color swatches use hexadecimal accessible labels instead of human color names.

## Recommended Target Layout

**Phone:** top bar = project, undo, redo, saved state, More. Compact side rail = Draw, Erase, Select, Add, Layers. One bottom sheet at a time. Draw sheet initially shows On model / In space, four brushes, color, and size; Advanced contains smoothing, pressure, snapping, opacity, and effects.

**Tablet landscape:** same top bar and side rail. One docked inspector on the side opposite the rail. A four-item contextual strip can sit near the bottom: placement, brush, color, size. Never show a second inspector or overlapping shelf.

**Tablet portrait:** retain the side rail; use one full-width lower sheet with a visible heading and close action. Keep the top half of the canvas usable. Prefer labeled rows over dense two- and four-column grids.

**Desktop:** preserve the same information hierarchy, then add keyboard shortcuts and optional labels. Do not create a separate, denser desktop product.

## Minor Observations

- “Add” is friendlier than “Create,” but component and panel terminology should use one label consistently.
- “Projects,” “Project Sessions,” and “Manage Sessions” should become one term, preferably **Projects**.
- “Plane,” “Canvas surface,” and “Drawing Plane” should become **Drawing surface**.
- A Guide selection currently routes users to Add when no guide exists; the choice should instead read **Add a guide…** so the navigation is predicted.
- Export should lead with outcomes: **Share an image**, **Save a 3D file**, **View in AR**; formats belong on the next step.

## Questions to Consider

- Is the five-tool rail (Draw, Erase, Select, Add, Layers) more important than keeping Erase in the contextual strip?
- Should advanced 3D controls remain available under **Precise controls**, or should some be removed from the hobbyist product entirely?
